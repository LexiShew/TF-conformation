# IDRs in DNA binding: the Barkai program and the conformational-ensemble angle

Across two decades of work the Barkai lab has moved the locus of transcription-factor (TF) binding specificity out of the folded DNA-binding domain (DBD) and into the intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) that flank it. The consequence for a structure-based specificity predictor is uncomfortable and specific. The sequence features that decide *where* a TF binds in vivo are, by the lab's own measurements, distributed, non-motif, and multivalent: precisely the features a single static protein–DNA complex cannot represent. That is the gap a conformational-ensemble front end is positioned to fill, and it is also where the project's risk concentrates.

## The experimental case

### Establishing the core claim

The foundational result is that long IDRs positioned away from the DBD are not passive linkers but the dominant in vivo targeting determinant. Using Msn2 and Yap1 — TFs from unrelated families, each with a long IDR outside its DBD — the lab found the IDRs both necessary and sufficient for localizing to most target promoters, and critically that this did not depend on any localized sub-domain but arose from many weak determinants spread across the entire IDR ([Brodsky et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2020.05.032)). The genome-scale generalization followed: comparing 48 budding-yeast TFs against their DBD-only, IDR-truncated, and IDR-only mutants showed that for nearly all of them the DBD alone bound different locations than the intact TF, with the difference mapping to disordered non-DBD regions ([Kumar et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2023.04.002)). The DBD-only mutants also lost the preference for fuzzy-nucleosome promoters that characterizes yeast regulatory architecture; the IDR, not the DBD, carries that bias.

Two competing explanations had to be eliminated for this to mean what it appears to mean. The first is cooperativity: that in vivo selectivity reflects co-binding of interacting TFs at proximal motifs rather than anything intrinsic to the IDR. Systematic co-depletion of TFs sharing promoters produced moderate effects confined to a promoter subset, with no case of mutual cooperation observed and Msn2 retaining binding at its preferred promoters even when fourteen similarly bound TFs were co-depleted ([Lupo et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2023.06.010)). A massively parallel binding assay across designed motif-cluster libraries then tested the model directly: occupancies were well explained by independent binding to individual motifs, with only limited motif combination effects ([Jana Lang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae846)). The second alternative, chromatin accessibility, was addressed in a hybrid-yeast allele-specific setting, where differential binding tracked motif sequence variation while accessibility differences between alleles had little apparent effect ([Krieger et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.276715.122)).

### A compositional, non-motif grammar

If IDRs direct binding, the sequence code must be readable. Profiling more than 100 designed Msn2-IDR mutants, some carrying up to 122 mutations within the 567-aa region, identified multivalent interactions carried by hydrophobic (mostly aliphatic) residues dispersed within a disordered background, independent of linear sequence motifs ([Jonas et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad184)). The sufficiency test is the strongest evidence that this grammar is real: de novo IDRs designed simply by dispersing hydrophobic residues among hundreds of hydrophilic ones, with no alignment-based similarity to any native TF, directed selective genome binding, and 185 such synthetic IDRs spanned a continuum of promoter preferences tunable by hydrophobic spread and disorder scaffold ([Liu et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2026.01.019)).

The code is not uniform, though, and this matters for how a predictor should be scoped. In Msn2, binding specificity and coactivator recruitment are encoded by interwoven grammars over the same >600-aa region: a relaxed, composition-based code for binding specificity and a more stringent, motif-based code for coactivator recruitment and transcription, such that mutations disrupting activity often left binding specificity untouched ([Mindel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad1191)). In Gln3, the two interaction types coexist within one IDR — short linear motifs plus a co-binding TF stabilize Gln3 at respiration-chain promoters, while binding at nitrogen-associated promoters is encoded by amino-acid composition independent of either ([Hurieva et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae521)). A single scoring function will not capture both regimes.

Composition also determines which DNA features an IDR responds to. Library-based measurement of TF binding to short tandem repeats within cells separated the two contributions cleanly for Msn2: the DBD bound motif half-sites while the 642-aa IDR favored homopurine–homopyrimidine and AT-rich repeats ([Vidavski et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2025.101349)). That is an IDR reading a DNA structural or compositional property rather than a base-readout motif: a shape-and-groove signal, which is the kind of feature a geometric model over a protein–DNA interface could in principle learn.

The mechanistic framing has evolved alongside. Early statements emphasized that distributing sensing determinants along an extended IDR accelerates target detection by localizing the TF to broad DNA regions around its sites ([Brodsky et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2020.05.032)), and the speed–specificity trade-off was posed explicitly as "distributed specificity" ([Jana et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tig.2020.12.001)). The current review keeps three candidate mechanisms live (condensate incorporation, TF co-localization, and direct IDR recognition of specific genomic regions) and is explicit that IDRs are hard to study because they confer specificity at low sequence complexity and stay functionally conserved while diverging rapidly in sequence ([Jonas et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-025-00816-3)).

### Scope and the mammalian test

The program is overwhelmingly budding yeast, which bears on generalization. The recent steroid-receptor work is the deliberate test: profiling three nuclear receptors with long disordered N-terminal domains confirmed that the NTDs direct binding across mammalian genomes, with NTD-deleted mutants shifting toward binding near AP-1 motifs while the intact NTD permitted binding at canonical receptor motifs far from AP-1 sites ([Zigdon et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280896.125)). Profiling the same receptors in budding yeast showed the NTD directs targeting even without its mammalian-specific interactors, which argues the effect is intrinsic to the disordered sequence rather than dependent on a partner proteome.

### Prior art: the ORC study

One Barkai-lab paper already ran the experiment the project proposes, on a smaller system, and its outcome is the most useful piece of prior art available. Mapping 38 designed Origin Recognition Complex mutants showed that a disordered basic patch (Orc1-BP4) and an insertion helix (Orc4-IH) guide binding differently at two motif classes, with a second basic patch (Orc1-BP3), absent from the cryo-EM structure entirely, able to substitute for Orc4-IH at the repetitive variant ([Chappleboim et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae249)). The structural work was done in collaboration with Yaakov Levy, whose group supplies much of the biophysics behind IDR-mediated DNA search.

The simulation result is the cautionary one. All-atom GROMACS runs (Amber99, TIP3P, steepest-descent minimization, 100-ps NVT and NPT equilibration, 1-µs production runs, 63 µs total) initiated from the DNA-bound cryo-EM configuration (PDB 5ZR1) scored mutants by the plasticity of BP4 localization, quantified as the standard deviation of RMSD relative to the cryo-EM pose. The simulations reproduced loss of confinement for the two direct base-contacting mutations (K362A, R367A), and were blind to the effects of residues a few positions away, eight of which retained DNA confinement in silico while the alanine scan showed experimental effects. The authors' preferred interpretation is that ORC–DNA binding depends on *transient* BP4–motif interactions that dominate the rate of reaching the high-affinity site visible to both the structure and the simulation, with a proposed model of basic patches sliding within the minor grooves of T-stretches and stabilizing at a non-T residue.

That is a direct statement of the failure mode facing this project: an ensemble anchored on the bound pose recovers the contacts already visible in the structure and misses the determinants that act before the complex forms. The lesson is not that dynamics don't help; it is that sampling *around the bound state* is the wrong sampling problem.

## Implications for the pipeline

### DeepPBS and BioEmu constraints

DeepPBS predicts a position weight matrix from a protein–DNA structure, representing the protein as a heavy-atom graph and the DNA as a base-symmetrized helix, with four bipartite geometric convolutions mapping protein atoms onto major-groove, minor-groove, phosphate, and sugar positions ([Mitra et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02372-w)). Several of its properties are favorable here. It accepts predicted as well as experimental structures, was benchmarked on RoseTTAFoldNA-folded complexes filtered at >500 contacts within a 5-Å cutoff, requires only a single forward pass with no MSA (which the authors note makes it suitable for analyzing MD trajectories), and is described as robust to small dynamical fluctuations while still responding to conformational change. Its minor-groove convolution channel is also the natural place for an Orc1-BP4-style disordered insertion or an AT-rich-repeat preference to register.

The constraints are equally clear. DeepPBS was trained on PDB complexes paired with JASPAR and HOCOMOCO PWMs — that is, on folded, motif-readout interfaces. A 645-aa IDR making dispersed multivalent contacts is out of distribution, and relative-importance scores are computed for heavy atoms within 5 Å of the symmetrized helix, so an IDR conformer that does not place residues at the interface contributes nothing. The model's output is a PWM over the DNA in the input structure, whereas the Barkai phenotype is *which* of many motif occurrences is bound: a promoter-selection readout, not a motif-preference readout. Mapping ensemble-averaged PWM output onto that phenotype is the project's central inferential step and should be stated as an assumption, not assumed to be free.

BioEmu is the right generator for the sampling side and should be used with its documented limits in view. It generates thousands of statistically independent samples from a protein's structure ensemble per hour on one GPU, trained on structures, over 200 ms of aggregate MD, and experimental stabilities, with relative free-energy errors around 1 kcal/mol against millisecond MD and measured stabilities ([Lewis et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.05.626885); [Nature Methods 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02874-1)). It is trained toward equilibrium ensembles of *foldable* proteins. A 645-aa low-complexity IDR is not what its training distribution rewards, and the one published attempt to use it as an ensemble prior found that the unweighted BioEmu pool average did not reproduce experimental SAXS scattering and required reweighting to become consistent ([Manori et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.70646)). Purpose-built IDR ensemble generators now exist and are worth benchmarking against it for the disordered segments specifically — STARLING generates IDR ensembles from sequence with environmental conditioning and Bayesian maximum-entropy refinement under experimental constraints ([Novak et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10141-2)), and IDPFold targets the same problem ([Zhu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202511636)).

The length distribution decides the architecture. Region lengths computed from the UniProt records of the lab's model TFs put Msn2's non-DBD region at 645 aa, Yap1's C-terminal IDR at 523 aa, Hsf1's at 387 aa, and Gln3's two IDRs at 305 and 400 aa, against 18 aa for Orc1-BP4 and 25–64 aa for the GATA and bZIP DBDs. A single whole-chain ensemble over 645 disordered residues will be dominated by global chain configurations that say nothing about interface geometry. Fragment-wise sampling (sliding windows over the IDR, each docked and scored, with per-window scores aggregated) matches both the tractable ensemble size and the lab's own finding that determinants are distributed rather than localized. It also means the mutational data are directly usable as a benchmark: the >100 Msn2-IDR mutants, the 185 synthetic IDRs, and the 38 ORC mutants are all published with genomic binding readouts.

Three design choices follow from the ORC precedent. Sampling should not be anchored on a bound pose, since that is exactly the regime where simulation recovered only the already-visible contacts; free-state ensembles docked against DNA, or unbiased search-state sampling, target the transient interactions the authors invoked instead. The scoring should be aggregate rather than best-pose, since a compositional grammar predicts that many weak conformer-level contacts sum to a preference: an ensemble-averaged PWM or a distribution over per-conformer scores, not the minimum-energy conformer. And energy minimization with liberal cutoffs, which the project already anticipates, is more defensible here than it would be for a folded interface, because the target quantity is a weak multivalent preference rather than a precise binding geometry. That same tolerance means a negative result will be hard to distinguish from insufficient sampling, which argues for fixing the benchmark before tuning the pipeline.

### Biophysics of IDR-mediated search

The Barkai measurements are in vivo and genomic; the mechanistic vocabulary comes largely from simulation work, much of it from Levy's group, and this is the literature that tells you what an ensemble is supposed to reveal. Disordered homeodomain tails were shown to destabilize the free protein by competing with native intramolecular interactions while stabilizing the DNA-bound state, reducing the folding–binding coupling and accelerating formation of specific interactions along the interface ([Tóth-Petróczy et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja9052784)). Coarse-grained simulations over 566 NK-2 and 55 Antp tail variants established that charge composition and its distribution along the tail, not merely net charge, set DNA search efficiency via intersegment transfer ([Vuzman & Levy 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1011775107)). Negatively charged IDRs, which are far more common than positively charged ones and which dominate the regions in the composition analysis above, act by electrostatically mimicking DNA and competing dynamically with it for positively charged DBDs: a dynamic autoinhibition that can *accelerate* target association in the presence of decoy sites ([Wang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad045); [Wang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.accounts.5c00261)). The most recent instance shows the negative Max tail shifting the dominant linear-diffusion mode from sliding to hopping via transient competition with the DBD ([Peer & Levy 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2026.169891)).

Experimental ensemble measurements say the same thing from the other side, and they bound what a static complex can encode. Integrating single-molecule FRET, NMR, and simulation for Sox2 showed that DNA and nucleosome binding induce major rearrangements in the C-terminal IDR ensemble *without* changing DNA-binding affinity, redistributing interdomain contacts and variably exposing two activation domains ([Bjarnason et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45847-2)). Solution NMR of the Sox2 DBD found the folded free protein in dynamic equilibrium with partially unfolded states that are quenched on specific binding, with overlapping specific and nonspecific interfaces ([Orsetti et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf1121)). This is conformational selection, in the regime BioEmu is actually good at. And a single-molecule interaction sensor in live cells recently found that an isolated Sp1 IDR could confer sharp locus specificity on an otherwise nonspecific DBD, with the authors proposing that specificity emerges when ensembles of unstructured interactions are scaffolded by transient DNA contacts ([Abidi et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeb6487)): an independent, mammalian, live-cell arrival at the Barkai conclusion, and a direct statement that the object of prediction is an ensemble rather than a complex.

One methodological result is worth folding into the pipeline design directly: in a Hox trimeric complex, minor-groove *width* reflected intrinsic DNA structure while minor-groove width *fluctuations* captured protein-induced reshaping ([Jiang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2026.03.036)). Fluctuation, not mean geometry, carried the induced-fit signal, an argument for scoring ensemble variance alongside the ensemble mean, and a reminder that the DNA side of the complex needs conformational treatment too, which neither BioEmu nor a fixed input helix provides.

### Open questions and a first milestone

The mechanism question is unresolved in the lab's own review: condensates, co-localization, and direct IDR–DNA recognition remain live alternatives ([Jonas et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-025-00816-3)). A structure-based pipeline speaks to exactly one of them. If ensemble-averaged interface scores predict IDR-directed promoter preferences, that is evidence for direct recognition; if they fail while the compositional grammar holds experimentally, that is evidence the effect is not encoded in interface geometry at all. Either outcome is informative, which is unusual and worth designing for explicitly.

The polymer-physics treatment supplies quantitative predictions to test against. Modeling a TF as a Rouse chain with one DBD site and multiple IDR sites yields an enhancement factor by which the IDR raises binding probability — always positive, but attenuated by the entropic penalty of pinning the chain — together with an "antenna" picture in which the TF binds a broad region and then diffuses effectively in one dimension to the target ([Ji et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.104956)). The number of interface-competent conformers in a fragment-wise ensemble is a structural proxy for the multivalency that model treats abstractly, so the two can be checked against each other.

Two gaps are worth naming plainly. First, no ensemble generator is validated for the specific object this project needs — a 600-aa disordered region in the presence of DNA. BioEmu is trained on foldable-protein equilibrium and STARLING on IDRs in isolation; neither models the electrostatic competition between a negative IDR and a DNA backbone that the Levy work identifies as mechanistically central. Second, the phenotype-to-prediction mapping is untested: no published work connects an ensemble-averaged PWM to in vivo promoter selection among many motif occurrences. Establishing that link on the ORC mutant panel (38 mutants, published binding data, a short disordered region within reach of exhaustive sampling, and an existing all-atom result to beat) is a better first milestone than Msn2, precisely because it is where the static approach is documented to have failed.
